Tracing: Shared-Stream Prediction, Development
Gating, and Closed-Loop Simulation
Phạm Triều Dương and Hồ Nguyễn Minh Sang
Faculty of Software Engineering
University of Information Technology, VNU-HCM
Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

Abstract—Graph-aware, uncertainty-aware knowledge tracing
and the distinction between predictive and instructional eval-
uation are established research directions. We conduct a nar-
rower audit of a frozen prerequisite-context/adaptation package
in Gain-Adaptive Memory Knowledge Tracing (GAM-KT), a
compact online learner-state estimator: does the package im-
prove predictions on a shared stream, which configuration is
selected by a development-only gate that includes an effective all-
pathways-off candidate, and does estimator replacement produce
a detectable difference in simulated outcomes under one frozen
selector? In a matched-structure simulator with three mastery
regimes and 50 untouched seeds, package-on Graph-GAM-KT
yields small Log-Loss gains over otherwise identical package-
off GAM-KT in the Low and Function-weak regimes (0.00319
and0.00169), with both remaining statistically detectable after
Holm correction. No Holm-corrected AUC difference is detected
in any regime, and no corrected direct difference is detected
in either primary closed-loop outcome: 14-day retained mean-
ability gain or mean learning gain. On disjoint-user Junyi
Academy logs, outcome-blind cycle removal is followed by a
300-user development gate that selects the predeclared effective
all-pathways-off null, represented by(s, α, w g) = (1,0,0)and
algebraically identical to package-off GAM-KT. The subsequent
1,000-user test run is therefore an implementation-identity check
rather than an estimate of a nonzero graph effect. The evidence
supports development gates that can disable every tested context
pathway and separate shared-stream estimator evaluation from
selector-dependent outcomes.
Index Terms—adaptive learning, knowledge tracing, online
evaluation, prerequisite graphs, student modeling
I. INTRODUCTION
Adaptive learning combines a learner-state estimator with
an instructional policy. Given historyH t−1 and itemq t, the
estimator predictsˆp t =P(y t = 1|H t−1, qt); a selector
then uses estimated state to choose a later activity. Prior
work has already shown that next-response accuracy alone
can be insufficient for evaluating an adaptive tutor [1], [2]. A
model may improve a proper scoring rule yet cross readiness
thresholds differently, alter the future item distribution, or fail
to improve a learning outcome.
Knowledge tracing (KT) ranges from interpretable BKT [3],
factor models [4], and educational Elo [5] to recurrent [6]
and graph-aware methods [7], [8]. Uncertainty-aware and
prerequisite-informed KT are also established [9]–[11]. We
therefore claim neither the first graph-aware or uncertainty-
aware estimator nor the first prediction–policy comparison.
Benchmarking work further shows that preprocessing and
target leakage can change KT rankings [12]. This motivates
strict predict-before-update ordering, but we treat it as an
information boundary rather than an algorithmic novelty.
Instead, we audit a frozen prerequisite-context/adaptation
package under three information boundaries.RQ1: in the
matched-structure simulator, on matched chronological event
streams, what incremental predictive value does the com-
plete package provide over otherwise identical package-off
GAM-KT?RQ2: on disjoint Junyi development users [13],
which prespecified graph configuration, if any, is selected
by mean sequential development Log Loss when the grid
includes an all-pathways-off candidate?RQ3: under one frozen
prerequisite-aware selector, does package-on Graph-GAM-KT
versus package-off GAM-KT produce a detectable difference
in simulated mean learning gain or 14-day retained mean-
ability gain?
Prior work already includes graph-aware, prerequisite-
aware, uncertainty-aware, and policy-aware learner models.
Our contribution is not a new general graph-KT paradigm;
it is an evaluation design combining (i) an all-pathways-
off development gate, (ii) a direct otherwise-identical shared-
stream estimator ablation, and (iii) selector-dependent closed-
loop contrasts under frozen information boundaries. This de-
sign yields conditional synthetic evidence of small package
increments without corrected primary-outcome differences and
a real-log gate that advances only the null.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Online state and uncertainty
BKT tracks binary mastery with initial, transition, guess,
and slip probabilities [3]; PFA models prior successes and
failures [4]; Elo provides inexpensive online learner–item
updates [5]; and LKT organizes recency, practice, learner,
and item features in an interpretable logistic framework [14].
SPARFA-Trace used approximate Kalman filtering for time-
varying concept knowledge well before this work [15]. UKT

more recently models predictive uncertainty explicitly [9].
Accordingly, GAM-KT’sπ c is only an internal, variance-like
update scale, not a calibrated posterior uncertainty.
GAM-RAG introduces an uncertainty-aware, Kalman-
inspired gain for evolving retrieval memory [16]. We cite it
only as the design origin of gain-adaptive memory, not as a KT
baseline, empirical evidence for education, or a priority claim
for Kalman learner models. Its relation-free retrieval index,
LLM feedback, and retrieval traces are absent here.
B. Structure and instructional policy
PDKT injects prerequisites into deep KT [7]; GIKT propa-
gates question–skill interactions [8]; and PSI-KT jointly mod-
els temporal knowledge and prerequisite structure [10]. KT-
DUSL studies uncertainty-aware domain-structure propagation
under high uncertainty or limited evidence [11]. Our narrower
distinction is procedural: outcome-blind cycle removal, an
explicit candidate with both graph signal and graph-dependent
adaptation disabled, a direct otherwise-identical shared-stream
ablation, and a selector-dependent closed-loop contrast.
Predictive models and instructional policies have long been
recognized as separate evaluation targets [1], [2]; robust offline
policy evaluation and decision-theoretic tutoring make the
decision layer explicit [17], [18]. We instantiate this distinction
for a development-gated prerequisite-context package rather
than present it as a new general gap.

The simulator inferential unit is the paired seed (n= 50per
regime). Each hypothesis uses a two-sided paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank test. Reported 95% intervals use 3,000 paired
seed-level bootstrap draws and estimate the mean raw paired
delta, not a median difference. Holm correction is applied once
across each complete 45- or 72-hypothesis family. Raw delta
is Graph-GAM-KT minus comparator: negative favors Graph-
GAM-KT for losses, errors, and prerequisite deficit, whereas
positive favors it for AUC, gains, soft mastery, coverage, and
route entropy.

V. RESULTS
A. RQ1: direct context-package increment
Table II isolates the frozen package-on minus package-off
estimator contrast, not a parent-only effect. All four probability
metrics improve in Low and Function-weak; only latent-
probability MAE survives correction in High. No regime
has a corrected AUC difference. The Log-Loss and Brier
increments are statistically detectable but small. Descriptively,
Graph-GAM-KT lowers Log Loss on 30/50 High, 50/50 Low,
and 49/50 Function-weak seeds. Against AdaptiveLMS on
the same fixed stream, Graph-GAM-KT lowers Log Loss
by.07478,.02870, and.01079, respectively; these larger
differences concern the full estimator, not the direct package
ablation.
As a simulator diagnostic, ranking the stochastic responses
by their generating probabilities yields mean AUCs of.5583,
.5555, and.5650. This is an oracle reference, not a strict finite-
sample ceiling, and helps explain why AUC is weak here.

For signal-only development candidates, Log Loss is

C. RQ3: estimator replacement in closed loop
Despite the corrected fixed-replay package increments in
Low and Function-weak, package-on Graph-GAM-KT versus
package-off GAM-KT has no Holm-corrected difference in
either primary outcome—14-day retained mean-ability gain
or mean learning gain—in any regime. This direct package
contrast is the primary RQ3 result. Comparisons with BKT
can differ because both the estimator family and induced route
change; their Elo-scale retained mean-ability-gain differences
are only.35–1.11and do not establish educational importance.
Table IV also shows that the uncertainty is large relative to
these direct outcome deltas. The experiment therefore fails to
detect a difference after correction under this selector; it does
not establish equivalence between the two systems.

outcome—14-day retained mean-ability gain or mean learning
gain—under this frozen selector. Because the package bundles
parent summaries with local-history modifiers, these data do
not attribute the increment to prerequisite edges alone. Junyi
gives a different but compatible result: development selects
the all-pathways-off null, so the test run is an implementation-
identity check only rather than another opportunity to tune on
test data.
The prediction–policy mismatch is neither new nor para-
doxical [1], [2]. Proper scores assess response distributions on
a shared stream; the fixed selector uses readiness, challenge,
coverage, and prerequisite constraints. Our evidence adds a
scoped case study: a context package should be evaluated
against the same estimator with every tested pathway disabled,
and any closed-loop statement must name both the package
and selector being held fixed.
VII. LIMITATIONS
Closed-loop evidence comes from one simulator, graph,
and inherited selector, not a classroom trial or a general
causal claim. The simulator contrast bundles parent summaries
with gated self-history modifiers, so it is not a pure parent-
edge ablation. The shared replay stream is oracle-generated
from latent ability rather than logged behavior. Exact reruns
depend on the released simulator code, inputs, and seeds;
the quick smoke test validates wiring but not the 50-seed
estimates. Junyi nodes are exercise proxies, and the supplied
relations need not be expert-valid prerequisites. Outcome-blind
removal of every edge inside a cyclic component can discard
valid directions. The 300-user point gate lacks a stability
analysis; its small differences do not prove positive weights
harmful. Macro and interaction-weighted Junyi Log Loss order
AdaptiveLMS and GAM-KT differently. The internal scales
πc are not calibrated posterior uncertainty. Complete coverage
rules out total concept omission, not all route collapse. The
descriptive baselines do not establish competitiveness with
standardized modern KT models; such comparisons and a pure
graph ablation require fresh held-out evidence.
VIII. CONCLUSION
This audit finds small shared-stream predictive incre-
ments from a frozen context/adaptation package in Low and
Function-weak, but no Holm-corrected AUC difference in any
regime and no corrected direct difference in either primary
closed-loop outcome—14-day retained mean-ability gain or
mean learning gain—under one frozen selector. The Junyi de-
velopment gate selects the predeclared effective all-pathways-
off null; consequently, the test run is an implementation-
identity check only, not a graph-effect test. The simulator
contrast does not isolate prerequisite edges. The defensible
contribution is therefore an evaluation pattern, not priority
over graph-aware, uncertainty-aware, or policy-aware KT: pro-
cess topology without outcomes, include an all-pathways-off
candidate, compare otherwise identical estimators on shared
streams, and evaluate selector-dependent outcomes separately.
